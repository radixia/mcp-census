# Shadow MCP — first join, 2026-08-05

Registry snapshot joined against the Phase 1 pilot census. **Not a release.**
One number here is solid, one is not, and the difference matters more than
either.

- **Registry:** official MCP Registry, full pull, 2026-08-05
- **Census:** the 501-domain Phase 1 pilot
- **Method:** `docs/DECISIONS/0003` — attribution by evidence only the domain
  owner could produce, never by string similarity alone

## The registry is bigger than the census side by three orders of magnitude

| | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Entries | 65,787 | |
| Under `io.github.*` (GitHub *account* verified) | 51,445 | 78.2% |
| Under a domain-verified namespace | 14,342 | 21.8% |

**78% of the registry is published under a GitHub account rather than a verified
domain.** That is the shadow ecosystem's shape in one number, and it is
independent of our census — it needs no join and no sampling to defend.

## Classification of the 485 brand domains

16 of the 501 pilot domains were excluded as not-brands: public suffixes
(`workers.dev`, `github.io`, `pages.dev`, `azurewebsites.net`, `myshopify.com`,
`service.gov.uk`, …) and RFC 2606 reserved names. They are everyone's
subdomain and nobody's brand.

| Class | n | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `official` | 2 | First-party server, **and** discoverable from the domain |
| `registered_undiscoverable` | 7 | First-party server exists, but no agent could find it from the domain |
| `orphan` | 3 | Discoverable card, nothing in the registry |
| `shadow_candidate` | 77 | Third parties named servers after the brand — **needs review, see below** |
| `absent` | 396 | Nothing |

## The solid finding

**Of the 9 brands with a provable first-party MCP server, only 2 are
discoverable from their own domain.**

| Brand | Evidence | Discoverable? |
|---|---|---|
| `cloudflare.com` | `com.cloudflare.mcp/mcp` | ✅ |
| `radixia.ai` | `ai.radixia/blog` | ✅ |
| `stripe.com` | `com.stripe/mcp` → `mcp.stripe.com` | ❌ |
| `paypal.com` | `com.paypal.mcp/mcp` → `mcp.paypal.com` | ❌ |
| `microsoft.com` | 18 verified namespaces incl. `com.microsoft/azure` | ❌ |
| `cloud.microsoft` | 10 endpoints on `agent365.svc.cloud.microsoft` | ❌ |
| `zoom.us` | 7 endpoints on `mcp.zoom.us` | ❌ |
| `oxylabs.io` | `io.oxylabs/oxylabs-mcp` | ❌ |
| `weather.com` | endpoints on `kulybaba.weather.com` | ❌ |

Stripe, PayPal, Microsoft and Zoom all operate MCP servers. **None of them can
be found by an agent that starts at their domain name.** The servers exist; the
front door does not.

This is defensible without any fuzzy matching: every row is proven either by the
registry's own DNS/HTTP namespace verification or by the endpoint being hosted
on the brand's own domain, which only the brand can do.

## The finding that is not ready

`shadow_candidate` is **not publishable as it stands**, and the count of 77
should not be quoted.

The signal is "a third party named a server after this brand" — matching the
brand token against the server-name segment. On distinctive brands it works:
`io.github.lanternrow/facebook-pages-mcp`, `com.mcparmory/shopify-admin`,
`io.github.codespar/mcp-paypal`, `ai.adramp/google-ads`,
`io.github.cunicopia-dev/ebay-mcp` are all real third-party wrappers.

On brands whose name is an ordinary word it collapses. Observed false positives,
all now suppressed by a stoplist:

- `unifi.it` — the University of Florence — matched Ubiquiti's UniFi servers
- `oracle.com` matched `token-oracle`, `discovery-oracle`
- `index.hr` matched `h-index`, `service-provider-index`
- `admin.ch` — the Swiss federal government — matched `shopify-admin`
- `guardian.co.uk` matched `import-guardian`, `tool-guardian`

A stoplist is whack-a-mole and locale-specific; it cannot be trusted to
generalise. Two problems also remain unfixed:

1. **No brand-level dedup.** `amazon.com`, `.co.uk`, `.de`, `.fr`, `.es`, `.it`
   are six rows for one brand, each carrying the same 18 matches.
2. **Sampled precision is roughly 85%**, from eyeballing 13 spread through the
   list. That is nowhere near good enough for a table that names companies and
   asserts who wrote their software.

The acceptance criterion for this phase was "eyeball 20 matches and agree with
them". On the current output we do not, so the phase is not complete.

## What would make it publishable

- Brand-level identity rather than per-domain, collapsing ccTLD variants.
- Corroboration beyond the name: require the entry to also reference the brand's
  domain in its description, repository or documentation URL.
- Manual review of every candidate that reaches the published table. At this
  scale that is tractable — tens of brands, not thousands.

## Framing discipline

A third-party server is not inherently malicious, and much of this list is
legitimate wrappers over public APIs. The claim the data supports is *"someone
else is defining how agents talk to your brand, and you do not know about it"* —
never *"you are under attack"*. That distinction is legal as well as editorial.
